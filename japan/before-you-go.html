Japan trip checklist: 10 things to sort out before you fly

For first-time visitors. Short on purpose. Facts checked Nov 16, 2026 against the official pages linked in each section — rules change, so follow the link when it matters.

1Fill in Visit Japan Web

Japan's government site for the immigration and customs forms. You enter your passport and flight details at home and get one QR code to show on arrival, instead of filling in paper cards on the plane.

  • It is free. Paper forms are still accepted if you skip it.
  • Every traveller needs their own entry, including children.
  • Open the QR code before you board — airport Wi-Fi can be slow.

Official: Visit Japan Web (Digital Agency)

2Arrange mobile data

You will lean on maps and train-route apps all day, so have data working when you land.

  • eSIM: bought online and installed before you fly, no shop visit. Check that your phone supports eSIM and is not locked to your carrier.
  • Pocket Wi-Fi: one device shared by a group, usually picked up and returned at the airport.
  • Roaming: the simplest and usually the most expensive — check your carrier's day-pass price.

Free Wi-Fi exists in stations, convenience stores and cafés, but it is patchy between them.

3Plan for some cash

Cards and phone payments work in most city shops, hotels and chains. Small restaurants, shrines and temples, markets and rural buses are often cash only.

  • ATMs that take overseas cards: 7-Eleven (Seven Bank), open 24 hours, and Japan Post Bank ATMs in post offices. Both have English menus.
  • Tell your bank you are travelling, and check your card's overseas withdrawal fee and daily limit.
  • Bring a coin purse. You will collect a lot of coins.

Official: Seven Bank · Japan Post Bank

4Get a transport IC card

Suica, PASMO, ICOCA and the other regional cards do the same job: tap in and out of trains, subways and buses in most cities, and pay at convenience stores and vending machines. One card works across regions.

  • iPhone: you can add a Suica in Apple Wallet, or use JR East's Welcome Suica Mobile app made for visitors (iPhone only; the card is valid for 180 days and the remaining balance is not refunded).
  • Physical card: sold at airport stations and major stations.
  • An IC card alone does not cover a Shinkansen trip — you still need a ticket.

Official: Welcome Suica Mobile (JR East)

5Check your medicines

Japan's rules are stricter than many countries'. Some medicines that are ordinary at home are restricted or banned, including certain stimulant, narcotic and psychotropic medicines.

  • Without paperwork you can bring up to a one-month supply of prescription medicine, and up to two months of ordinary non-prescription medicine, for your own use.
  • More than that needs an Import Confirmation (Yunyu Kakunin-sho), applied for before you travel — allow a couple of weeks.
  • Medicines containing amphetamine or methamphetamine cannot be brought in at all, even with a prescription. Narcotic medicines need separate permission in advance.
  • Keep medicines in the original packaging and carry a copy of the prescription.
  • If you take anything for ADHD, strong pain, sleep or anxiety, check the official guidance for that exact medicine before you fly.

Official: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare — bringing medicines for personal use · Narcotics Control Department

6Measure your suitcase for the Shinkansen

On the Tokaido, Sanyo and Kyushu Shinkansen (the Tokyo – Kyoto – Osaka – Hiroshima – Hakata – Kagoshima route), luggage over 160 cm in total (length + width + height) needs a seat with oversized-baggage space.

  • Booking that seat is free when you reserve your ticket. Turning up without one costs a fee.
  • Easier: send big bags ahead by luggage delivery (takkyubin) from your hotel or the airport, and travel with a day bag.

Official: JR Central — oversized baggage

7Know the new tax-free rules

Since Nov 1, 2026 you pay the full price including tax in the shop and get the tax back when you leave Japan. It is no longer taken off at the register.

  • Shop at stores with a Tax-Free sign and show your actual passport, not a copy.
  • Keep the goods and receipts until you depart; customs may ask to see them.
  • Older guides and videos describe the previous system.

The step-by-step is in the tax-free how-to inside Japan Pocket Helper.

8Check your dates against the busy seasons

Trains and hotels fill up, and prices rise, around:

  • New Year: about Dec 29 – Jan 3. Many shops, restaurants and museums close.
  • Golden Week: late April to early May.
  • Obon: mid-August.
  • Cherry blossom (late March – early April) and autumn leaves (November), in Kyoto especially.

If your trip overlaps, reserve Shinkansen seats and popular restaurants early.

Official: Cabinet Office — national holidays (in Japanese)

9Pack the right plug

  • Sockets take two flat pins (Type A), the same shape as North America. Three-pin and round-pin plugs need an adapter.
  • Voltage is 100 V. Phone and laptop chargers marked “100–240 V” are fine; some hair tools are not.
  • A power bank is worth carrying — a day of maps and photos drains a phone.
