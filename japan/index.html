What is Japan Pocket Helper?

Japan Pocket Helper is a free pocket tool for travellers in Japan who do not speak Japanese. Instead of trying to pronounce Japanese travel phrases, you pick a situation and show the card on your phone to the person in front of you — a waiter, a station attendant, a taxi driver, a pharmacist or a hotel clerk. Each card shows clear, polite Japanese in large letters, with the English meaning underneath so you always know what you are showing.

It is made in Japan, works offline after your first visit, and needs no account and no download from an app store.

How to use it in 3 steps

  1. Pick a situation — eating out, trains and buses, taxi, hotel, shopping, health, or everyday basics. Or type a word such as “toilet”, “allergy” or “receipt” in the search box.
  2. Tap a card and show this card to the staff. The Japanese fills the screen. Tap “Flip” to turn it towards the other person, or “Speak” to have your phone read it aloud.
  3. Let them answer by pointing. Question cards show “Yes / No / I don't know” in Japanese and English, so staff can simply point to the answer.

Situations it covers

  • Restaurants: a table for two, an English menu, “what do you recommend?”, the bill, paying separately, and asking whether a place is cash only or takes cards.
  • Food allergies and diets: build your own allergy card for Japan. Choose from 12 common allergens such as egg, milk, wheat, peanuts, shellfish and soba (buckwheat), or things you avoid such as pork, alcohol, meat or fish, and show one clear card to the kitchen.
  • Trains and buses: “does this train go to…?”, which platform, where to buy a ticket, and topping up an IC card such as Suica.
  • Taxis and addresses: save your hotel's address in Japanese once, then show a “please take me here” card to the driver.
  • Hotels and shopping: keeping your luggage until check-in, tax-free shopping, sizes, gift wrapping.
  • Pharmacy and clinic: point at your symptoms — fever, headache, stomach ache, rash and more — and show your own details card with allergies, medicines and an emergency contact.
  • Emergencies: one-tap calls to 110 (police) and 119 (ambulance and fire), the Japan Visitor Hotline, earthquake basics, and what to do if you lose your passport or leave something on a train.

More than phrases

The How-to tab has short guides to things that confuse first-time visitors: using IC cards, Shinkansen seats and the oversized luggage rule, coin lockers and luggage delivery, getting cash from ATMs that accept overseas cards, the new tax-free refund rules, onsen and public baths, and visiting shrines and temples. The Manners tab gives quick do's and don'ts for trains, restaurants, money, baths and more. A simple yen converter shows prices in your own currency using the rate you enter.

Your privacy

Anything you type — your hotel address, your allergies, your personal details, your search words — is stored only on your phone and is never sent to us. The website counts visits and which cards are opened, anonymously, so we can improve the cards.
