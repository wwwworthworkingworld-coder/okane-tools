Final tax return in Japan: who files, when and how

The basics of the kakutei shinkoku for foreign residents, straight from the National Tax Agency (NTA). Checked 2026-11-17.

When

The normal filing period is February 16 to March 15 of the following year (NTA page on the final tax return). For example, income earned in 2026 is filed in 2027. The NTA announces the exact dates each year; check them before you file.

Who has to file

The NTA states a general rule: you file if your total income, after deductions, leaves a tax larger than the credits already applied at year-end adjustment. It also lists exceptions. In outline:

  • Salary earners usually do not need to file if all of these apply: salary totals 20 million yen or less, it comes from one payer, tax was withheld on all of it, and other income (excluding salary and retirement income) is 200,000 yen or less.
  • Public pension recipients usually do not need to file if benefits total 4 million yen or less, tax was withheld on all of it, and other income is 200,000 yen or less.
  • If you are not required to file, you can still file a refund return when the tax withheld or prepaid is more than the tax for the year. This includes people who left a job during the year without a year-end adjustment, and people claiming deductions such as the medical expense deduction or donation deductions. A refund return can be filed for up to five years.

Whether you must file depends on all your income. The NTA's list has more conditions than shown here. Check the NTA pages or ask the tax office.

The NTA's English guide

The NTA publishes an English PDF, "You can create a Final Tax Return at Home!", a simplified step-by-step input manual for the 2025 return (filed in 2026). It is also available in Chinese, Vietnamese, Portuguese and Nepali. The online tool it points to (keisan.nta.go.jp) is in Japanese. Look for the next edition before the next filing season.

Filing online with e-Tax: what you need

According to the NTA, to use e-Tax you need one of these:

  • A My Number Card with an IC card reader/writer, or a compatible smartphone; or
  • An electronic certificate on a smartphone (available from January 2025); or
  • An ID and password issued by the tax office.

You can also print the forms and mail them. File with the tax office that covers your address at the time of filing.
