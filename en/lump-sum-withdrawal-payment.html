Japan pension lump-sum withdrawal payment

Who can claim it, what to send, what it costs you, and how to get the 20.42% tax back. Based on the Japan Pension Service guide and the National Tax Agency, checked 2026-11-17.

Who can claim

You can claim if you meet all of these (Japan Pension Service, JPS):

  1. You do not have Japanese nationality.
  2. Your National Pension contribution-paid period or Employees' Pension Insurance enrollment period is 6 months or more.
  3. You do not have an address in Japan.
  4. You have never been eligible to receive a pension (including a disability allowance).

You must claim within two years of the date you no longer had an address in Japan. If you did not submit a moving-out notification to your municipality, you are still treated as insured, and you cannot claim until your re-entry permit expires.

Think before you claim

  • If you have 120 months (10 years) or more of eligibility period, you cannot claim the lump-sum, because you can receive the Japanese old-age pension later.
  • If you have less than 120 months, you can claim — but claiming erases all Japanese pension coverage periods before the claim.
  • Some countries have a Social Security Agreement with Japan that lets you add up your periods. See the JPS list: countries with agreements.

Documents to send

  • The claim form: "Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment Claim Form (National Pension/Employees' Pension Insurance)".
  • Copy of your passport (the page with name, date of birth, nationality, signature and residence status).
  • Proof that you no longer have an address in Japan (for example, your deleted residence record). Not needed if you submitted the moving-out notification before you left, because JPS can confirm it.
  • Bank document showing the bank name, branch name, branch location, account number and that the account holder is you.
  • A document showing your Basic Pension Number (Pension Handbook or Basic Pension Number Notice).

The English form, an Excel version and an example of a completed form are on the JPS page below.

How the amount is set

  • It depends on your months of coverage in Japan, up to a cap of 60 months (5 years) (36 months if all your periods end before April 2021).
  • National Pension: a fixed amount by months. For a last paid month between April 2026 and March 2027 it runs from ¥53,760 (6–11 months) to ¥537,600 (60 months or more).
  • Employees' Pension: your average standard remuneration × a rate. The rate is half the insurance rate × a number of months, rounded to one decimal.

The 20.42% tax and how to get it back

  • For the Employees' Pension lump-sum, 20.42% income tax (including the Special Income Tax for Reconstruction) is withheld at payment, because you are a non-resident when you are paid. The National Pension lump-sum is not withheld.
  • You can get the withheld tax back by filing the "Tax Return for Refund Due to Taxation on Retirement Income at the Taxpayer's Option" with the tax office that covers your last address in Japan.
  • To file and receive the refund from abroad, you must appoint a tax agent in Japan and submit the "Notification of Tax Agent for Income Tax/Consumption Tax" before you return to your country. If you did not, submit it together with the return.
  • JPS says there is no special qualification for a tax agent other than having an address or place of residence in Japan.
