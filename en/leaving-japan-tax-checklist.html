Leaving Japan: tax and pension checklist

What to sort out before you leave, in a sensible order. General information, checked against official pages on 2026-11-17.

The short version

  1. Decide whether you will claim the pension lump-sum. Claiming erases all your Japanese pension periods. Read this first.
  2. Choose a tax agent (a person or company with an address in Japan) before you leave, if you will still have tax matters to handle. How it works.
  3. Submit the moving-out notification to your city or ward office. The pension claim and resident tax both depend on it.
  4. Deal with resident tax — it is tied to where you lived on January 1. Your options.
  5. File your income tax return for the year you leave, either before departure or through your tax agent (see the timeline below).
  6. Claim the lump-sum from abroad within 2 years of leaving, then the tax refund through your tax agent.

Income tax: two ways to leave

The National Tax Agency (NTA) describes these cases for residents, and for non-residents with income subject to aggregate taxation, who leave Japan while they still have tax matters to settle:

CaseWhat the NTA says
You leave without naming a tax agentFile a final return with the tax office that covers your address, and pay the tax before you depart.
You leave after naming a tax agentFile the final return and pay by the statutory due date. If the notification was submitted before departure, the return and payment go through the agent, normally between February 16 and March 15 of the following year.

Sources: NTA pages 12004 and 12021 (below). Late filing or payment can add extra tax and delinquent tax.
