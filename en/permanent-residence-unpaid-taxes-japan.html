Permanent residence and unpaid taxes, pension and insurance

What the Immigration Services Agency (ISA) guideline says about the new revocation ground for "intentionally not paying public taxes and charges". Plain-English summary of the official text, checked 2026-11-17.

What changed

The 2024 revision of the Immigration Control Act added three grounds on which the status of permanent resident (eijusha) can be revoked: not complying with duties under the Act, intentionally not paying public taxes and charges, and a prison sentence for certain listed crimes. National and local government staff can also report suspected cases to the ISA. The ISA published a guideline that explains how it reads these grounds.

Effective date: law-firm and press summaries say the new grounds start in April 2027, but the ISA guideline page we read does not state a date. Confirm the date on the ISA website.

What counts as "public taxes and charges"

  • Taxes: for example income tax, resident tax, corporate tax and property tax.
  • Public charges: for example public health insurance premiums (National Health Insurance, employees' health insurance) and public pension premiums (National Pension, Employees' Pension).
  • Not included: fees and user charges, and fines or penalties imposed for a specific act (the guideline gives traffic fines as an example).
  • "Not paying" is not only about arrears. It also covers not paying what should have been paid because you clearly had to file a return and did not.
  • If a company is taxed and you actually manage it and know about the payment duty, the guideline says not paying can count too.

What "intentionally" means

The guideline says the ground is aimed at malicious non-payment, not at the bare fact that something is unpaid. It means you knew you had a duty to pay and deliberately did not. The ISA looks at everything together: income and assets, the number, amount and length of arrears, the payment record, how the arrears arose, and your conduct. It applies when it is clear that you have no intention of paying. It does not apply where there are unavoidable circumstances and you are not at fault. It does not set a single amount that triggers it.

The guideline's examples of cases that may countExamples that are not expected to count
Long arrears, and you moved without telling the local government so that you cannot be foundYou cannot pay because of illness, disaster, job loss or similar unavoidable reasons
You ignore repeated reminders and collection steps and never show that you will payYou responded to reminders, showed willingness to pay and are on an installment or deferral arrangement
You repeatedly miss the deadlines of an installment or deferral arrangementYou receive support equivalent to public assistance

What happens if a ground applies

  • Meeting a ground does not mean automatic revocation. The ISA considers how it came about, your later record and your intentions, and hears from the person concerned.
  • Unless the Minister finds that you should not stay in Japan, the Minister changes your status on the Minister's own initiative to a status other than permanent resident. The guideline says this will be "long-term resident" (teijusha) in most cases. You can apply for permanent residence again once you meet the requirements.
  • Revocation of status altogether is expected where payment is clearly not going to happen, for example a clear unwillingness to pay, repeated arrears, or a conviction for tax evasion.

Practical points from the official wording

  • The guideline treats responding and arranging payment (installments, deferral) differently from ignoring notices. If you are struggling to pay, the place to ask is the office that sent the bill: city or ward office (resident tax, National Health Insurance, National Pension), tax office, or pension office.
  • It also names not filing a return you clearly had to file. See the final tax return guide.
  • If you are leaving Japan, see the leaving-Japan checklist.
This summarizes an official guideline in general terms. It does not decide whether any ground applies to you or what you should do.
