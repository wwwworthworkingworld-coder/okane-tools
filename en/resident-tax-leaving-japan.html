Resident tax when you leave Japan

Resident tax (juminzei) follows where you lived on January 1, not where you are now. Here is what the official guides say.

The January 1 rule

  • Resident tax is a local tax, charged by the city or ward where you were registered on January 1, whatever your nationality.
  • It is based on last year's income. So a bill can arrive after you have left.
  • Cities state that you must pay it even if you move within Japan or abroad after January 2.
  • Whether you owe anything depends on your income and situation. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications says it applies to foreigners who have a domicile in Japan on January 1 and earned income above a certain amount; a tax treaty may remove the obligation in some cases.

Your usual options before you leave

OptionWhat city guides say
Pay in full before you leaveSome cities issue a payment slip (nofusho) if you tell the resident tax division you are leaving.
Appoint a tax agentThe agent receives your notices and pays on your behalf. Cities such as Nagoya list this as the alternative.
Employer deductionIf you are employed, ask whether the remaining amount can be taken from your final pay in one sum.

The exact rules differ by city. Ask your own city or ward office.

Do not skip the moving-out notice

A city guide warns that if your permitted stay runs past January 1 of the next year and you leave without a moving-out notification, you may still be charged resident tax even though you no longer live in Japan.

Notices usually arrive around mid-June (for example, Sendai says so). The amount and your obligation depend on your own case — ask your city office.
